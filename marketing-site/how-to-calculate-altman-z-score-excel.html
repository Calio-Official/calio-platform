How to Calculate the Altman Z-Score from SEC 10-K Filings in Excel

Published October 2026 • Sourced from US-GAAP SEC XBRL Taxonomy

The Altman Z-Score, developed by NYU Stern Finance Professor Edward Altman in 1968, remains one of the most reliable multi-factor formulas for predicting corporate distress, credit degradation, and bankruptcy risk within a two-year horizon.

While many commercial terminals present the Z-Score as a black-box number, institutional equity and credit analysts calculate it directly from audited Balance Sheet and Income Statement line items. Here is the exact formula, US-GAAP XBRL mapping, and Excel implementation:

Public Manufacturing Formula (Altman Z-Score)
Z = 1.2(X1) + 1.4(X2) + 3.3(X3) + 0.6(X4) + 0.999(X5)

Where each X-ratio measures liquidity, cumulative profitability, operational productivity, market leverage, and asset turnover.

Z-Score Interpretation Zones & Risk Thresholds

Safe Zone
Z > 2.99

Company exhibits pristine balance sheet liquidity, low leverage, and minimal likelihood of distress.

Grey Zone
1.81 ≤ Z ≤ 2.99

Moderate vulnerability. Requires close inspection of debt maturity schedules and cash conversion cycles.

Distress Zone
Z < 1.81

High probability of insolvency or restructuring within 24 months. Warning signal for credit investors.
