1. The 3 Core Connection Mechanisms

In an integrated financial model, changes in operational drivers propagate across all three statements simultaneously. The workbook enforces the three fundamental accounting bridges:

Link 1: Net Income

P&L → Cash Flow → Equity

Net Income from the bottom of the Income Statement serves as the top row of Cash Flow from Operations (CFO), and flows into Retained Earnings on the Balance Sheet.

Link 2: Depreciation & CapEx

PP&E Roll-Forward Schedule

CapEx increases Property, Plant & Equipment (PP&E) under Cash Flow from Investing (CFI), while D&A reduces PP&E and is added back to CFO as a non-cash expense.

Link 3: Ending Cash

Cash Balance Reconciler

Net change in cash from operating, investing, and financing activities reconciles into Cash & Cash Equivalents at the top of the Balance Sheet, ensuring Assets = Liabilities + Equity.

2. Workbook Structure & Sheet Hierarchy

To prevent messy cross-sheet formulas, the model follows an institutional tab layout:

Tab Name Core Function Primary Accounting Formulas
1. Income Statement P&L revenue, gross margin, operating expenses, tax, net income Gross Profit = Revenue - COGS; EBIT = Gross Profit - OpEx
2. Balance Sheet Assets, liabilities, shareholder equity balance verification Total Assets = Total Liabilities + Total Equity
3. Cash Flow CFO (operating), CFI (investing), CFF (financing) cash flows Ending Cash = Beginning Cash + CFO + CFI + CFF
4. Debt & Interest Maturity schedules, revolving credit line, interest expense Interest Expense = Average Debt × Effective Interest Rate
